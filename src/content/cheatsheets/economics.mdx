## The one identity

Everything in this module is one line, used three ways.

$$
\underbrace{\text{GPU-seconds per request}}_{m} = t_{pre} + \frac{n_{out}\, t_{step}}{B}
$$

- **Prefill** you own alone — one parallel pass over your prompt, compute-bound.
- **Decode** is shared: one step serves all $B$ sequences, so your share is $t_{step}/B$.

Then dollars, two ways:

$$
\text{\$/M output} = \frac{m \times (p/3600) \times 10^6}{n_{out}}
\qquad
\text{fleet} = \left\lceil \frac{\lambda \times m}{\rho} \right\rceil
$$

$p$ = dollars per GPU-hour · $\lambda$ = requests/s · $\rho$ = target utilization.

**Same $m$, two questions:** divide by $n_{out}$ for price per token, multiply by $\lambda$ for fleet size.

## The two terms

| | $t_{pre}$ | $t_{step}$ |
|---|---|---|
| Bound by | **FLOP/s** (compute) | **bandwidth** (bytes) |
| Time | $\max\frac{2Pn}{\text{flops}\cdot m_{fu}},\ \frac{W}{\text{bw}\cdot m_{bu}} + 2\text{ms}$ | $\max\frac{W + B\cdot c\cdot k_{kv}}{\text{bw}\cdot m_{bu}},\ \frac{2PB}{\text{flops}\cdot m_{fu}} + 1.5\text{ms}$ |
| Shared? | No | Yes, across the batch |
| Batching helps | **Barely** | **A lot** |

Achievable bandwidth fractions $m_{bu}$: about 0.80 for BF16, 0.75 for FP8, 0.65 for INT4. Achievable compute fraction $m_{fu} \approx 0.55$.

## Bytes, the cheapest lever

$$
\text{weights} = P \times b, \qquad
\text{KV/token} = 2 \cdot L \cdot H_{kv} \cdot d_h \cdot b_{kv}
$$

Llama-3.1-8B BF16: 16.1 GB weights, **128 KB/token KV**. At 2,048 ctx that is 262 MB of KV per stream.

$$
\text{max streams} = \frac{\text{mem} - \text{weights} - 0.04\,\text{mem}}{\text{KV/token} \times c}
$$

**8B on an 80 GB card, BF16:** 226 streams at 2,048 ctx · 905 at 512 · 56 at 8,192. Context length moves concurrency by >10×.

## Output tokens cost multiples of prompt tokens

Same config (8B, H100, batch 64, 2,048 ctx):

| Shape | \$/M prompt | \$/M output | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 512 in / 256 out | 0.023 | 0.20 | **8.5×** |
| 2,048 in / 64 out | 0.021 | 0.83 | **39×** |
| 128 in / 1,024 out | 0.043 | 0.16 | 3.6× |

**The ratio is not constant — it is your prompt-to-answer ratio.** Cheapest levers: cap `max_tokens`, shorten prompts, cache shared prefixes.

## Choosing a GPU

$$
\text{goodput per dollar} = \frac{\text{goodput (tok/s)} \times 3600}{p}
$$

Two prices per card, and **they rank differently**:

| Card | \$/hr | \$ per (TB/s) → decode | \$ per (PFLOP/s) → prefill |
|---|---|---|---|
| T4 | 0.35 | 1.09 | 5.38 |
| L4 | 0.80 | 2.67 | 6.61 |
| A100 80GB | 1.60 | 0.80 | 5.13 |
| H100 SXM | 2.50 | 0.75 | 2.53 |
| H200 | 3.50 | 0.73 | 3.54 |
| B200 | 5.50 | 0.69 | 2.44 |

- A100 → B200: bandwidth price falls **14%**, FLOP price falls **52%**. New hardware helps prefill far more than decode.
- **Break-even test: a cheaper card wins if it is proportionally slower than it is cheaper.** Compute both factors *per user*, not per batch.
- Worked (8B, 512/256, 2,048 ctx, 64 streams): A100/H100/H200/B200 span **3.3× in throughput, 1.06× in value**. When the spread is under ~10%, choose on fit and operational risk.
- On **prefill-bound** traffic the spread is much larger — the newest card does win. Compute the spread for your workload before generalising.

## When it does not fit

- 70B BF16 = **141 GB** → 2 cards minimum, bill ×2, plus ~15% per extra rank for the all-reduces.
- **Precision first, then card.** 70B INT4 = 35 GB → **1 card** at \$1.35/M output vs \$6.65 for BF16 on two H100s — **5× cheaper**.
- Never price a configuration that would OOM. It is arithmetic about a machine that does not exist.

## Capacity planning

$$
\text{busy GPUs} = \lambda \times m \quad\text{(a floor)} \qquad
\text{fleet} = \left\lceil \frac{\lambda m}{\rho} \right\rceil
$$

Worked (8B, H100, 64 streams, 512/256, $m = 0.073$ GPU-s):

| Rate | Busy | Fleet @60% | \$/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 req/s | 0.15 | 1 | 1,825 |
| 12 req/s | 0.87 | 2 | 3,650 |
| 30 req/s | 2.18 | 4 | 7,300 |

- **Run at 50–70%.** The unused 30–50% is the queue having somewhere to go.
- **The bill is a step function of fleet size**, not of utilization. Utilization moves within a step; only card count moves between steps.
- **Headroom = committed capacity**, not a utilization target. Autoscaling on utilization gives a floor, never a ceiling in time. Scale on queue depth or p95 latency.
- **Utilization tax:** at 40% average utilization you pay **2.5×** per unit of useful work.

## Re-run the plan when

Model changes · precision changes · prompt/output length distribution shifts · engine version moves · **traffic crosses the last step you planned** (the expensive one).

## Reporting rules

1. Every cost number states **model, GPU, precision, concurrency, and both length distributions**. A cost per token without its workload is a rumour.
2. Keep **derived / measured / structural** columns apart and label every cell (Module 17's discipline).
3. Eliminate on **hard constraints first** — does it fit, does it meet the SLO. Missing the SLO eliminates; it does not score badly.
4. Report **goodput per dollar**, not throughput and not dollars per million tokens alone.
5. Report the fleet as **floor + committed + headroom reason + scaling trigger**.
6. Ship a **sensitivity section with thresholds**. A decision without one is a preference.

## Managed APIs, cloud platforms & build vs buy

**Six rungs, least control to most:** (1) foundation-model API → (2) one-click open-model endpoint → (3) bring your own weights → (4) bring your own code → (5) bring your own image → (6) build your own stack.

| Rung | You own | Provider hides | You pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 API | nothing | batching, cache, autoscaling, cold starts | per token |
| 2 One-click | model + instance choice | container, engine, scaling defaults | per instance-hour |
| 3 BYO weights | model | engine, kernels, KV policy, scaling | per instance-hour |
| 4 BYO code | model + request logic | base image, drivers, cluster | per instance-hour |
| 5 BYO image | model + engine + kernels + KV policy | cluster, node autoscaling, endpoint routing | instance-hour + storage |
| 6 Build | all seven layers | nothing | GPU-hours + people + failures |

**BYO-container contract:** HTTP server on the routed port · readiness endpoint returns 200 only after weights load and warm-up · invocation endpoint accepts the request schema · weights on a mounted path, not in the image · clean SIGTERM shutdown. Tie to **Warm-up, health checks & graceful shutdown**.

**Self-hosted marginal cost per million output tokens:**

$$
c_{\text{self}} = \frac{p/3600}{G \times u} \times 10^6
$$

$p$ = \$/GPU-hour · $G$ = measured output tok/s at the SLO · $u$ = average utilisation over the month (idle hours included).

**With fixed operations $O$ per month and $V$ daily tokens:**

$$
c_{\text{self}}(V) = \frac{p \times 10^6}{3600\,G\,u} + \frac{O \times 10^6}{30.42\,V}
\qquad
V^* = \frac{O \times 10^6}{30.42\,(c_{\text{api}} - c_{\text{self}})}
$$

**Illustrative worked example:** \$2.50/GPU-hour, 4,603 tok/s, 60% utilisation, \$0.60/M API, \$1,800/month ops.

- Marginal: \$0.000694/s ÷ 2,762 tok/s × 1e6 = **\$0.251/M**.
- Gap: \$0.60 − \$0.251 = \$0.349.
- Crossover: 1,800 × 1e6 ÷ (30.42 × 0.349) ≈ **170M tokens/day**.

| Average $u$ | tok/s | Marginal \$/M | Crossover M/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% | 4,603 | 0.151 | 132 |
| 80% | 3,682 | 0.189 | 144 |
| 60% | 2,762 | 0.251 | 170 |
| 40% | 1,841 | 0.377 | 265 |
| 30% | 1,381 | 0.503 | 609 |
| 25% | 1,151 | 0.604 | none |

**Rules of thumb**

- **Headroom and idle hours push the crossover right.** At 25% average utilisation the marginal self-hosted token is already above a \$0.60/M API, so no volume wins.
- **TCO is the bill:** GPU-hours × cards kept warm + on-call + monitoring + spare/failed GPUs + storage/egress. A warm spare roughly doubles the fixed line.
- **The real bill is a step function** of card count (Module 25.3), not the smooth curve used for planning.
- **Non-cost filters first:** governance/privacy, latency control, custom models/fine-tunes, lock-in, rate limits, compliance. A hard constraint eliminates a rung; it is not a score.
- **Decision defaults:** prototype → rung 1–2; regulated enterprise → rung 3–5 in a private network (or 6); steady high volume → rung 5–6; spiky low volume → rung 1–2.
- **Seven layers:** public API (M23/M24) · resource management (M25/M23) · model selection (M23/M17) · distributed serving (M18/M20/M21) · core inference (M09/M17) · model optimisation (M10/M11/M14/M16) · model (M04). Rung 1 takes all seven; rung 3 leaves the model; rung 5 leaves the bottom three; rung 6 leaves all seven to you.
- **Why build still matters:** read the provider's limits, benchmark on your workload at your SLO, and recompute the crossover when prices, utilisation, model or compliance change.
