## The two limits

$$
t \ge \max\left(\frac{F}{\text{peak FLOP/s}},\ \frac{B}{\text{bandwidth}}\right)
$$

$$
\text{arithmetic intensity} = \frac{F}{B}\ [\text{FLOP/byte}] \qquad
\text{ridge} = \frac{\text{peak FLOP/s}}{\text{bandwidth}}
$$

- Below the ridge: **memory-bound**. Buying arithmetic does nothing.
- Above it: **compute-bound**. Buying bandwidth does nothing.

## Memory hierarchy

| Level | Size (H100) | Latency | Managed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registers | 256 KB per SM | ~1 cycle | compiler |
| L1 / shared | 256 KB per SM | ~30 cycles | you |
| L2 | 50 MB | ~200 ns | hardware |
| HBM | 80 GB | ~600–700 ns | you |

## Hardware shape

- Warp = 32 threads, one instruction, lockstep. Branches inside a warp run **all** paths.
- H100: 132 SMs × 4 tensor cores = 528 tensor cores. 4 processing blocks per SM, each with 1 scheduler + 32 FP32 lanes + 1 tensor core.
- Coalescing: a warp's 32 loads gather into 128-byte lines. Contiguous = 1 fetch; scattered = up to 32.
- Occupancy = resident warps per SM, limited by registers (256 KB) and shared memory.
- Kernel launch ≈ 5–10 µs. Fuse small kernels.

## Intensity of each LLM operation

| Operation | Arithmetic intensity |
|---|---|
| Elementwise (SiLU, add) | ~0.3 |
| KV cache write | ~0.5 |
| **Decode GEMM (m = 1)** | **~1** |
| Attention, context T (Llama-8B BF16) | **T / 8** |
| Prefill GEMM (m = 512) | ~410 |

For `[m × 4096] · [4096 × 4096]` in BF16:

$$
F = 2m \cdot 4096^2, \qquad B = (4096^2 + 2m \cdot 4096)\times 2
$$

Intensity grows almost exactly linearly in $m$: the weight bytes are constant.

## The two moves

Every optimization in this course is one of:

1. **Raise intensity** — batch, reuse in cache or registers, use a better kernel.
2. **Lower bytes** — quantize, fuse, compress, reuse the KV cache.

## The stack

```
your code → PyTorch / ATen / Inductor → CUDA runtime → NVIDIA driver → GPU
```

- CUDA is asynchronous. `torch.cuda.synchronize()`, or CUDA events, before you time.
- Errors name the wrong layer: a driver "illegal memory access" is usually your bug, four layers up.
- Remote GPU: ~20–80 ms round trip, container on someone else's host, disk is not shared.
- `nvidia-smi`; `torch.cuda.get_device_properties(0)`.

## Other accelerators

The roofline is brand-neutral:

$$
\text{ridge} = \frac{\text{peak FLOP/s}}{\text{bandwidth}}
\qquad
t_{\text{decode}} \ge \frac{\text{weight bytes}}{\text{bandwidth}}
$$

| Accelerator | Memory | Bandwidth | Peak FP16/BF16 | Ridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H100 SXM | 80 GB HBM3 | 3.35 TB/s | 989 TFLOP/s | ~295 |
| MI300X | 192 GB HBM3 | 5.3 TB/s | 1,307 TFLOP/s | ~247 |
| MI325X | 256 GB HBM3E | ~6.0 TB/s | 1,307 TFLOP/s | ~218 |
| TPU v5p | 95 GB HBM | ~2.76 TB/s | 459 TFLOP/s | ~166 |
| Inferentia2 | 32 GB HBM | ~0.82 TB/s | ~190 TFLOP/s | ~232 |
| Gaudi 3 | 128 GB HBM2e | ~3.7 TB/s | 1,835 TFLOP/s | ~496 |
| Groq LPU | 230 MB SRAM | ~80 TB/s on-chip | ~188 TFLOP/s | ~2.4 |
| Cerebras WSE-3 | 44 GB SRAM | ~21 PB/s on-chip | ~125 PFLOP/s | ~6 |

All approximate public figures, at the time of writing.

**Worked decode.** Llama-3.1-8B, FP16, 16.1 GB of weights:

- H100: 16.1 GB ÷ 3.35 TB/s ≈ 4.8 ms → 209 tokens/s at 100% of peak.
- MI300X: 16.1 GB ÷ 5.3 TB/s ≈ 3.0 ms → 330 tokens/s at 100% of peak.
- At 80% of peak: about 167 and 264 tokens/s.
- Ratio = bandwidth ratio 5.3 ÷ 3.35 ≈ 1.58, not the FLOP ratio 1.32.

**SRAM capacity.** 230 MB per chip:

- 8B FP16: 16.1 ÷ 0.23 ≈ 70 chips.
- 70B FP16: 141.2 ÷ 0.23 ≈ 614 chips.
- 70B at 8-bit: 70.6 ÷ 0.23 ≈ 307 chips.
- 70B at 4-bit: 35.3 ÷ 0.23 ≈ 153 chips.
- One 192 GB MI300X holds 70B FP16 in one package; a 44 GB Cerebras wafer would need about 3.2 wafers.
- Per-chip SRAM read: 0.23 GB ÷ 80 TB/s ≈ 2.9 µs. The links and the small batch become the limit.

## The software moat

| What changes | What stays |
|---|---|
| Kernels and fused operators | The roofline and ridge point |
| Quantisation formats and engine backends | Arithmetic intensity decides the bound |
| Collective library and interconnect | KV cache size and bandwidth maths |
| Profiler, graph capture, debugging | Prefill vs decode and batching |
| Supported dtypes and architectures | The SLO and goodput you target |

- CUDA: cuBLAS, cuDNN, FlashAttention, CUTLASS, TensorRT-LLM, vLLM, SGLang, AWQ/GPTQ/FP8, NCCL, Nsight.
- ROCm: AMD’s CUDA counterpart; `torch.version.hip`; RCCL.
- XLA/JAX: Google TPU compiler and front end; pods use a dedicated interconnect.
- Neuron SDK: AWS Trainium and Inferentia compiler/runtime.

## Evaluate honestly

$$
\text{cost per million tokens} = \frac{\text{price per hour}}{\text{tokens/s} \times 3600} \times 10^6
\qquad
\text{tokens/s per watt} = \frac{\text{tokens/s}}{\text{watts}}
$$

- Compare at your SLO, on your model, context, concurrency and precision.
- Use **goodput**: requests per second that meet the SLO.
- Include host, interconnect, cooling and idle time; use wall power, not chip TDP.
- Worked example: 167 tokens/s, \$2.50/hour, 700 W → 167 × 3600 = 601,200 tokens/hour; \$2.50 ÷ 0.6012 ≈ \$4.16 per million tokens; 167 ÷ 700 ≈ 0.24 tokens/s per watt. Illustrative inputs, not a quote.
- Peak TOPS is usually INT8 or sparse. Decode is memory-bound and often BF16/FP16, so peak TOPS can be irrelevant.
