## Where the bits are

Two numbers dominate everything. Activations are rounding errors next to them.

| | count | bytes | Llama-3.1-8B in BF16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weights | $P$ | ${}2P$ | 16.06 GB |
| KV cache / token | ${}2LH_{kv}D$ | ${}2\cdot2LH_{kv}D$ | 131,072 B = 128 KiB |
| Activations / token | ~${}5d_{model}$ | ~${}10\,d_{model}$ | ~41 kB — negligible |

## The algorithm

$$
q = \operatorname{clamp}\!\big( \operatorname{round}\big(\tfrac{v-z}{s}\big) + z,\ 0,\ 2^b-1\big),
\qquad \hat{v} = s\,(q - z)
$$

$$
s = \frac{\max|v|}{2^{b-1}-1}, \qquad
|\hat v - v| \le \frac{s}{2} = \frac{\max|v|}{2(2^{b-1}-1)}
$$

| bits | levels | grid step for max=0.61 | worst-case error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 256 | 0.0048 | 0.4% |
| 4 | 16 | 0.0871 | 7.1% |
| 3 | 8 | 0.2033 | 16.7% |
| 2 | 4 | 0.6100 | 50% |

## Granularity

| scheme | scales for an $m\times n$ matrix | 4-bit error |
|---|---|---|
| Per tensor | 1 | worst — outliers delete small weights |
| Per channel | $m$ | good |
| Per group $g$ | $\lceil n/g\rceil m$ | best per byte |

$$
\text{overhead} = \frac{32}{g\,b}, \qquad
\text{effective bits} = b + \frac{32}{g}
$$

| group $g$ | 4-bit overhead | effective bits | 8-bit overhead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 25.0% | 5.00 | 12.5% |
| 64 | 12.5% | 4.50 | 6.25% |
| **128** | **6.25%** | **4.25** | 3.13% |
| 256 | 3.13% | 4.13 | 1.56% |

Group 128 at 4 bits is the production default. "4-bit" means 4.25.

## Error accumulation

Independent per-layer errors add in quadrature:

$$
\varepsilon_{\text{out}} \approx \varepsilon\sqrt{L}
$$

| bits | per-weight | after 32 layers | after 80 layers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.3% | 1.7% | 2.7% |
| 4 | 6% | 34% | 54% |

Worst case only. Real measured error is several times smaller because errors are structured, not random.

## The decode step

$$
\text{bytes} = \underbrace{P\cdot\tfrac{b_w}{8}\cdot\Big(1+\tfrac{32}{g b_w}\Big)}_{\text{weights}}
+\underbrace{B\,T\cdot 2LH_{kv}D\cdot\tfrac{b_{kv}}{8}}_{\text{KV}}
$$

$$
t_{\text{step}} = \max\!\Big(\tfrac{\text{bytes}}{\text{BW}\cdot\text{MBU}},\ \tfrac{2PB}{\text{FLOP/s}\cdot\text{MFU}}\Big) + \text{overhead}
$$

Llama-3.1-8B, $B{=}32$, $T{=}4096$, H100 (2.68 TB/s achieved):

| Config | weights | KV | bytes | step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 / BF16 | 16.06 | 17.18 | 33.24 GB | 12.4 ms |
| W4 g128 / BF16 | 4.27 | 17.18 | 21.45 GB | 8.0 ms |
| FP8 / FP8 | 8.03 | 8.59 | 16.62 GB | 6.2 ms |
| **W4 g128 / KV8** | 4.27 | 8.59 | 12.86 GB | **4.8 ms** |
| W4 g128 / KV4 | 4.27 | 4.29 | 8.56 GB | 3.2 ms |

Compute the ratio first:

$$
\frac{\text{KV bytes}}{\text{weight bytes}} = \frac{B\,T\cdot 2LH_{kv}D\cdot(b_{kv}/8)}{P\,(b_w/8)}
$$

Small batch + long context → KV dominates → quantize the cache first.
Large batch + short context → weights dominate → quantize the weights first.

## Choosing a scheme

| Scheme | W | A | Tensor cores | Prefill | Decode | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 | 16 | 16 | BF16 | 1× | 1× | none |
| FP8 | 8 | 8 | FP8 (Ada, Hopper, Blackwell) | up to 2× | ~1.5× | low |
| W8A8 | 8 | 8 | INT8 | up to 2× | ~2× | moderate |
| **W4A16** | 4 | 16 | BF16 (after dequant) | **1×** | ~3× | low |
| W4A16 unfused | 4 | 16 | BF16 | **slower** | **slower** | — |

- **W4A16**: weights only. 4× less weight memory, ~3–4× shorter decode step, prefill unchanged.
- **W8A8**: activations too, so INT8 tensor cores engage. The prefill/throughput lever.
- **FP8**: per-element exponent, no group scales, no metadata, outlier-tolerant. 2× tensor-core rate on Ada Lovelace (L4, L40S, RTX 4090), Hopper and Blackwell; memory-only win on Ampere (A100) and T4.
- **A4/A8 activation quantization** needs a per-token dynamic scale, fused into the preceding kernel.

Choose in this order: **bottleneck → hardware → quality budget → kernel support.**

## The three methods

| | RTN | AWQ | GPTQ | SmoothQuant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Key idea | quantize each weight alone | protect salient channels | push rounding error forward | move activation outliers into weights |
| Signal used | none | $\lVert W_{:,j}\rVert\lVert X_{:,j}\rVert$ | $H^{-1} = (XX^\top)^{-1}$ | per-channel activation max |
| Calibration | none | ~128 seqs | ~128 seqs | ~512 seqs |
| Exact? | — | no | no | **yes** (algebraic identity) |

**AWQ**: salient = $\lVert W_{:,j}\rVert\lVert X_{:,j}\rVert$; 1% of channels often carry a large share. Give them a finer grid, not more bits.
**GPTQ**: quantize column-by-column; after column $i$, spread $\delta_i$ onto later columns weighted by $H^{-1}$. Greedy, forward-only → order matters (blocks, act-order).
**SmoothQuant**: $\hat X_{\cdot,j} = s_j X_{\cdot,j}$, $\hat W_{j,\cdot} = W_{j,\cdot}/s_j$. Output unchanged in exact arithmetic. $\alpha \approx 0.5$–$0.8$ for W8A8.

Compose them: **AWQ scaling as preprocessing, GPTQ as quantizer** is the common best-quality default.

## Kernels

Fused or nothing:

```
fused:     load packed tile → shared memory → widen to BF16 in registers → tensor cores
unfused:   read packed → WRITE BF16 copy → read it back → matmul   (moves MORE bytes than BF16)
```

## KV cache

| bits | bytes/token (8B) | grouping | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 | 131,072 | — | default |
| FP8 E4M3 | 65,536 | none needed | best default on Ada, Hopper, Blackwell |
| INT8 | 65,536 | per-token | safe, portable |
| INT4 | 32,768 | per-token or per-head | real cost at long context |
| INT2 | — | — | do not |

Error accumulates with sequence length: 8,000 quantized keys all shift slightly. Symptom is **repetition and lost long-range recall**, not gibberish.

**Always sweep context length when the KV cache is quantized.**

## Measuring quality

$$
\text{PPL} = \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac1N\sum_i \log p(x_i\mid x_{<i})\Big), \qquad
\text{SE} \approx \sqrt{\tfrac{p(1-p)}{n}}
$$

| samples | 95% interval |
|---|---|
| 200 | ±6.8 pts |
| 1,000 | ±3.0 pts |
| **5,000** | **±1.4 pts** |
| 20,000 | ±0.7 pts |

A 4-bit regression is 1–4 points → you need thousands of samples. Use **paired McNemar** (5–10× more sample-efficient).

Failure signatures:

| signature | cause |
|---|---|
| repetition | KV quantization, long context |
| lost needle | KV quantization |
| late hallucination | 4-bit weights + greedy decoding |
| near-miss code | 4-bit weights (MLP) |

## The decision

**Domination:** A dominates B if A ≥ B on every axis and > B on one. Delete the dominated points; the survivors are the frontier.

Quality index ≈ ${}1 - (\varepsilon_w + 0.6\,\varepsilon_{kv})\sqrt{L}$.

Pick by **quality budget**:

- SLO already met at BF16 → most aggressive config inside budget.
- Throughput-constrained → fastest config inside budget.
- Long-context → W4 + FP8 KV; INT4 KV usually out.

Write the **falsifier**: one sentence naming what would move you off your choice.

## Distillation & pruning

### The three levers

| | What changes | Who | Training pipeline? | Quality risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantization | number format | serving team | no | 1–4 pts at 4 bits |
| Pruning | which weights exist | model team | usually (recovery FT) | moderate–high |
| Distillation | the model itself | training team | yes | a few pts, hard tasks |

Quantization is the default because it is a config change, not a training project. The other two are worth it only when you need a **fundamentally smaller** model.

### Distillation

$$
p_i = \frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)},
\qquad
L_{\text{soft}} = -\sum_i q_i \log p_i = H(q) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\,\|\,p)
$$

Teacher logits 3.0, 1.0, 0.0:

| T | top | second | third |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.844 | 0.114 | 0.042 |
| 2 | 0.629 | 0.231 | 0.140 |
| 4 | 0.481 | 0.292 | 0.227 |

$$
\frac{\partial L_{\text{soft}}}{\partial z_j} = \frac{p_j - q_j}{T}
\;\approx\; \frac{z_j - s_j}{nT^2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{multiply the soft loss by } T^2
$$

- $H(q)$ is constant, so the student minimises the **KL** — distribution matching, not answer-guessing.
- Hard label = argmax only, zero gradient on the alternatives. Soft label = the whole vector.
- Worked: teacher $[0.629, 0.231, 0.140]$ vs student $[0.556, 0.276, 0.168]$ → soft loss 0.917 nats, $H(q)$ = 0.906, trainable KL = **0.011 nats**.

**Sequence-level** (no logits needed): prompts from your traffic → sample teacher outputs → filter with a verifier or judge → supervised fine-tune. You need the outputs and a licence that allows training on them, not the weights. Filter, or the student learns the teacher's mistakes.

**Public example, at the time of writing:** DeepSeek-R1 (671B MoE) → R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B/14B/32B and R1-Distill-Llama-8B/70B. Approximate published scores for the 70B student vs teacher: MATH-500 94.5 vs 97.3, GPQA Diamond 65.2 vs 71.5, LiveCodeBench 57.5 vs 65.9.

### The serving arithmetic

$$
t_{\text{step}} \approx \frac{P\,b_w/8}{\text{BW}\cdot\text{MBU}}
\qquad
\text{tok/s} \approx \frac{\text{BW}\cdot\text{MBU}}{P\,b_w/8}
$$

H100: 3.35 TB/s × 0.8 MBU = **2.68 TB/s**. Effective: $t \approx \text{weight bytes} / 2.68\text{e}12$.

| Option | Weights | Fits 80 GB? | tok/s, one stream |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70B BF16 | 141.2 GB | no (2 cards) | 19 (if it fit); ~38 on 2 cards |
| 70B INT4 (4.25 b) | 37.5 GB | yes | 71 |
| 8B BF16 | 16.1 GB | yes | 167 |
| 8B INT4 | 4.27 GB | yes | 628 |

KV per token: 70B (80 layers) = **327,680 B = 320 KiB**; 8B (32 layers) = **131,072 B = 128 KiB**. 2.5× more context for the same GB.

**Order:** evaluate an existing distilled model → quantize the big model → distill only if quantization cannot reach the target → prune without recovery fine-tuning, never.

Distillation cost, derived: 8B student × 20B tokens × 6 FLOP/param/token = 9.6e20 FLOPs; 8×H100 at 40% MFU ≈ 3.2e15 FLOP/s → ~85 h ≈ 680 GPU-hours ≈ a few thousand dollars. Quantization is one GPU for an afternoon.

### Pruning

| | Unstructured | 2:4 | Structured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Removed | single weights | 2 of every 4 | heads / channels / layers |
| Shape | unchanged | unchanged + 2-bit index | **smaller** |
| Dense-kernel speed | none | none | **proportional** |
| Sparse-hw speed | only with kernels | **up to 2× math** | not needed |
| Decode | no gain | no gain unless compressed | **real gain** |
| Quality cost | lowest | low | highest |

A zero is not free until something skips it: dense layout still stores, reads and multiplies it.

Arithmetic intensity decides where sparsity pays:

| Phase | FLOP/byte | Bound | 2:4 helps? |
|---|---|---|---|
| decode, batch 1 | 2 / 0.53 ≈ **3.8** | memory | no |
| H100 ridge | ≈ **200** | — | — |
| prefill, 4k tokens | 8,000 / 0.53 ≈ **15,000** | compute | **yes, up to 2×** |

2:4 storage: 2 values + a 2-bit index per 4 weights → 8.5 bits/weight at FP16 (about half), 4.5 at INT4 — before scale metadata.

Recovery fine-tuning: prune → continue training on a sample of the original data. Always. Published 2:4 checkpoints (e.g. Sparse Llama 3.1, vendor-reported, at the time of writing) are the output of that process.

### Measuring the drop

Same harness as quantization: PPL → tasks from your own traffic → length sweep if the KV shape changed → report with a decision and a rollback.

Three differences for distillation:

1. It can **improve** a task (trained on data like your eval). Keep the golden set out of the training data.
2. Compare against **two** baselines: the teacher, and the model in production now.
3. **Prompt format is part of the model.** Evaluate with the exact chat template and sampling settings you will serve.

## The three sentences

1. Decode reads every weight and all the KV cache every step, so **bytes per step is the latency** — and quantization is the only lever that moves it directly.
2. At 4 bits the grid step is set by outliers and errors accumulate as $\sqrt{L}$, so **granularity and calibration are not polish, they are the method**.
3. Always measure quality at your **longest served context** with **enough samples** to resolve 2 points, and pick the least aggressive config that meets your speed target.
