## The three kinds of metric

| Type | Behaviour | Read it as | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Counter** | only increases | `rate()` over a window | requests, output tokens, retries, preemptions |
| **Gauge** | up and down | the value, as a range | running, waiting, KV blocks used, GPU memory |
| **Histogram** | accumulates buckets | a quantile, later | TTFT, TPOT, end-to-end |

$$
\text{rate} = \frac{\text{increase over the window}}{\text{window length}}
$$

- **Never store a latency mean.** Store buckets, **pool buckets across replicas**, then take the quantile. Percentiles are not decomposable — a fleet p95 is not the average of per-replica p95s.
- Put a **bucket edge exactly at your SLO threshold**, so "missed the SLO" is a count, not an interpolation.

## The four panels

1. **Rate and goodput** — output tok/s next to goodput. Throughput alone is a vanity number.
2. **Latency shape** — p50/p95/p99 TTFT and TPOT as *time series*. A number on a board hides a regression.
3. **Pressure** — running, waiting, queue depth, admissions rejected. The **leading** indicator.
4. **Engine health** — decode step time, KV blocks used, preemptions, cache hit rate.

Order matters: panels 3 and 4 lead, panels 1 and 2 lag.

## Cardinality

$$
\text{series} = \text{buckets} \times \prod_{\text{labels}} |\text{values}|
$$

Worked: 12 buckets × 2 models × 8 replicas × 250 tenants = **48,000 series for one metric**.

- **Multiplicative**, never sublinear. Every new label multiplies.
- Never label with `request_id`, `user_id`, free text, or `pod_name`. Identifiers belong in **logs and traces**.

## Error budget and burn rate

$$
\text{burn rate} = \frac{\text{fraction missing}}{\text{fraction allowed}}
$$

SLO p95 TTFT < 600 ms over 30 days → 5% may miss → budget 0.5%.

- Burn 1.0 = on budget. Burn 2.4 = a month gone in 13 days. Burn 14.4 = a month gone in 2 hours.
- Alert on **two windows** (5 min + 1 h → page; 1 h + 6 h and 6 h + 3 d → ticket). Burn rate pages; the percentile dashboard explains.

## Traces

- Span = **name, service, start, duration, parent**. Nest everything.
- Span **components**, not kernels (~300 spans/request is unqueryable).
- **Always span the queue wait** — invisible elsewhere, and the most common cause of a bad p95.
- **Critical path** = spans with no gaps. Its total is the latency; anything off it cost nothing to fix first.
- Mint one `trace_id` at the edge, propagate to the last hop, echo the engine id back.
- Streaming: bind context to the **request**, never to the connection or the thread.
- Sample: **tail** (keep slow + failed). Head sampling discards the case you built traces for.

## Capacity and the overload math

$$
\text{capacity} = \frac{\text{concurrency slots}}{\text{mean service time}}
$$

Worked: 24 slots, mean 1.89 s (62% × 0.8 s + 30% × 2.5 s + 8% × 8.0 s) → **12.7 req/s**.

$$
W_{\text{queue}} = \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\times S
$$

| $\rho$ | queue wait | | $\rho$ | queue wait |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | 1× S | | 90% | 9× S |
| 80% | 4× S | | 95% | 19× S |

- **A slot is held from prefill to the last token.** One 128k prompt is a capacity attack.
- **Burst budget:** waiting room $= \text{excess} \times \text{tolerable wait}$. $(60 - 12.7)\times 30 = 1{,}419$ excess → a 2 s budget needs 2,838 slots. A room that large also permits multi-minute waits.
- **Unbounded queues are never right**: they convert fast rejections into indefinite waits at full cost.

## Admission control and shedding

Checks, in order, all O(1): identity + quota → shape (`max_model_len`, `max_tokens`) → capacity (free slot, room not full) → priority.

- **Shed when serving one request would cause more requests to miss the SLO than it would make succeed.**
- Threshold shedding needs **hysteresis**: resume at ~80% of the shed level, or the system oscillates.
- Policies: threshold (simple, cliff) / latency-aware (targets the SLO) / cost-aware (fairer, complex).
- Fairness: FIFO starves long requests; shortest-job-first maximizes goodput but excludes long-context users; **weighted fair queuing** is the only one where "we shed fairly" is true.
- **Scale on queue depth or latency, never on request rate** — otherwise autoscaling fights the shed policy.

## Timeouts

$$
t_{\text{hop}} = \frac{\text{deadline} \times (1 - \text{margin})}{\text{hops}}
$$

Worked: 2.0 s deadline, 5 hops, 20% margin → **320 ms per hop**.

- **Every timeout shorter than its caller's remaining budget**, or the caller's deadline is decorative.
- The margin covers call-graph branches you did not enumerate.
- Streaming needs an **idle** timeout (no bytes for N s = dead), not a total timeout.

## Retry amplification

$$
\mathbb{E}[\text{attempts}] = 1 + p + p^2 + \dots + p^{r}
$$

| Case | $p$ | $r$ | factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent, mild | 0.05 | 2 | **1.05×** |
| Independent, bad | 0.20 | 2 | **1.24×** |
| Correlated (backend down) | ≈ 1 | 2 | **3×** |
| Four layers, dead backend | ≈ 1 | 2 each | **64×** (or 256× at four) |

- Retries **multiply across layers**. Put them at **exactly one layer**.
- **Retry budget:** at most $N \times (R-1)$ retries, $R \le 2$. 1,000 requests at $R = 1.25$ → 250 retries. Bucket empty → return the error.
- Retry only failures where **the server did no work** and the failure is plausibly independent. 503/504/429 yes; a 500 from a compute kernel no; a timeout on a non-idempotent write no without an idempotency key.

## Circuit breakers

Closed → (failure threshold over a **time** window) → Open → (cooldown, **doubling**) → Half-open (**1 probe**) → Closed.

- Too eager: a 2 s blip becomes a 30 s outage. Too reluctant: the threshold trips only after the damage.
- One breaker per **dependency**; health-aware routing per **replica**.

## Degradation order

Cap `max_tokens` → drop to FP8 → shorten context → smaller model → cache-only → (last) refuse.

**Fast error beats slow success. Near-correct answer beats both.**

## Chaos testing

1. Write the hypothesis. 2. One blast radius. 3. Inject one fault. 4. Watch the four panels. 5. Record time-to-detect and time-to-recover.

Five experiments: kill mid-stream · timeout above deadline · retries up at two layers then kill the backend · fill KV to 95% and double load · throttle the engine link to 100 Mbit/s.

**Recovery:** add jitter everywhere (or you get a thundering herd), ramp the breaker closed gradually, and confirm **goodput back at baseline** — not error rate at zero.

## Security for an inference endpoint

It is a **compute tenant**, not a web page. Three questions:

| | Risk | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| **Confidentiality** | whose data can leak | tenant in cache key + KV namespace, zero slots on free, log redaction, TLS |
| **Integrity** | what is actually serving | pin model **digests** not tags, verify on load, immutable deploys, signed images |
| **Availability** | can I be made to stop | per-key quotas, admission control, `max_model_len`, budgets |

- **Availability is the most exposed**: 24 slots ÷ 8 s = **3 req/s** of pure capture = 10,800 requests/hour, for free to the attacker.
- **Authenticate at the edge only**; the engine is internal. Per-key token-rate **and** concurrency quotas.
- **Never let the client name the model.** Reference a deployment by id.
- **Weights are exfiltratable** by repeated sampling — so rate limits and output caps are *confidentiality* controls too.
- **Allow-list telemetry**, never redact-list: prompts, `Authorization`, error bodies and user ids leak by default.

## The four-question threat model

1. If our biggest tenant vanished, what is our burn rate?
2. If someone sent 10,000 × 128k-token prompts, what stops them? Name the control **and the number**.
3. Could a new engineer read another tenant's prompts? Follow the cache key and the block namespace.
4. Where does a prompt exist in plaintext? Logs, traces, backups, the block pool.

## The runbook

1. **SLO** — metric, percentile, threshold, window.
2. **Admission** — pool, waiting room, derivations, shed response (status, body, `Retry-After`).
3. **Timeouts and retries** — deadline, per-hop, max attempts, budget ratio, **which single layer retries**.
4. **Recovery** — readiness includes warm-up; gate on goodput at baseline.
5. **Blast-radius limits** — per-tenant concurrency, per-key token rate, `max_model_len`, with numbers.
