Standing example: **Llama-3.1-8B in BF16 on one 80 GB H100.** 32 layers, 8 KV heads, head dim 128. Spec numbers are approximate public figures.

## The step model

$$
t_{\text{step}} = \max(t_{\text{weights}} + t_{\text{KV}},\ t_{\text{math}}) + t_{\text{overhead}}
$$

$$
t_{\text{weights}} = \frac{M \cdot w}{\text{bw} \cdot \text{MBU}}
\qquad
t_{\text{KV}} = \frac{B \cdot c \cdot k}{\text{bw} \cdot \text{MBU}}
\qquad
t_{\text{math}} = \frac{2 \cdot M \cdot B}{\text{FLOP/s} \cdot \text{MFU}}
$$

$M$ params, $w$ bytes/weight, $B$ batch, $c$ context, $k$ bytes KV per token per sequence.

**The weight term does not grow with $B$. That single fact is the whole module.**

## Memory

$$
k = 2 \cdot L \cdot H_{kv} \cdot D_h \cdot b = 131{,}072 \text{ B} = 128 \text{ KiB}
$$

$$
\text{KV per seat} = c \cdot k
\qquad
\text{seats} = \frac{\text{budget}}{c \cdot k}
$$

| Llama-3.1-8B, BF16 | Bytes |
|---|---|
| Weights | 16.06 GB |
| KV per token | 128 KiB |
| KV per seat @ 2,048 | 256 MiB |
| KV per seat @ 8,192 | 1.0 GiB |

Marginal return collapses past ~128: 1→8 seats is **7.3×** throughput for a 9% slower step; 128→196 is **1.15×** for a 34% slower step.

Prefill on the same GPU: 512 tok ≈ 17 ms · 2,048 tok ≈ 63 ms · 8,192 tok ≈ 244 ms.

## Dynamic batching (classic servers)

$$
B_{\text{expected}} \approx \min\left(B,\ 1 + \lambda D\right)
$$

- The window opens with the first request and closes at B requests or after the oldest has waited D.
- Added wait ≤ D; mean ≈ D/2 when the window closes on time rather than on size.
- λ = 200/s, D = 5 ms → λD = 1 → expected batch 2. With s(n) = 1.0 + 0.5n ms: 1.5 ms alone (667 req/s) vs 2.0 ms for 2 (1,000 req/s); latency 1.5 → about 4.5 ms.
- D = 10 ms → batch 3, 2.5 ms, 1,200 req/s. The cap B = 8 fills at λ ≥ 1,400/s and tops out near 1,600 req/s.
- Triton: `dynamic_batching` with `max_batch_size`, `preferred_batch_size`, `max_queue_delay_microseconds` (Module 17).
- Right for fixed-shape classifiers, embedders and rerankers. Wrong for generation: a batch is a stream and still ends with its slowest member, so the tail survives → continuous batching.

## The engine loop

```
every step:
  1. retire    finished / cancelled / preempted free their seat NOW
  2. schedule  fill freed seats from the waiting queue, in policy order,
               checking the KV budget (not just the seat count)
  3. step      one forward pass; every running sequence emits one token
```

Never blocks. Never waits for a group.

## Two wastes — do not confuse them

| Waste | Cause | Killed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Tail waste** | seat held by a finished request | continuous batching → 0 |
| **Fill waste** | no request waiting to fill the seat | more traffic, not more seats |

$$
\text{static waste} = 1 - \frac{\sum_i \ell_i}{n \cdot \max_i \ell_i}
\qquad
\text{fill waste} = 1 - \frac{\bar{B}}{\text{seats}}
$$

Measured, 12 ragged requests at 6 seats: static 316 steps / 38% seat waste; continuous 271 steps / **0%** seat waste, average batch 4.4 of 6.

## The three knobs

| Knob | Bounds | Start at |
|---|---|---|
| `max_num_seqs` | sequences decoding at once | derive from the KV budget |
| `gpu_memory_utilization` | fraction of HBM the engine claims | 0.90 |
| `max_num_batched_tokens` | tokens processed in one step | 2,048 |

At 0.90 of an 80 GB H100: budget $= 0.9 \times 80 - 16.06 - 3.2 = 52.7$ GB → **196 seats @ 2,048 ctx**, **49 @ 8,192**. Size it on *typical* context, never the maximum.

## Diagnose before tuning

| avg batch | queue | Diagnosis | Do |
|---|---|---|---|
| low | empty | **Starved** | more traffic; seats are not the constraint |
| at limit | deep | **Saturated** | shorten the step; more seats will hurt |
| high | deep | **Prefill storm** | lower `max_num_batched_tokens`; chunk the prefill |
| high | deep, TPOT↑ | contexts too long | cap context; FP8 KV halves it |

## Queue policies

| Policy | Rule | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| FIFO | arrival order | a long request blocks everyone behind it |
| Priority | priority first | sustained high-priority load starves the rest |
| Shortest-first | shortest job first | long requests starve |
| Aging | priority rises with wait | — (this is the cure for starvation) |

- **Head-of-line blocking**: a 200-step request holds its seat for 200 steps; a request arriving at step 5 waits 195.
- **Cancellation** is free: free the seat the step the client disconnects.
- **Preemption** evicts a sequence and **replays every token it had streamed**. Evict the *newest* — it has generated the least. It is a safety net against OOM, not a source of throughput (forcing it cost 19% wall-clock and 77 recomputed tokens for no gain).

## Rules of thumb

- The first ~8–32 seats are nearly free; past ~128 at 2K context, seats are expensive.
- Continuous batching removes the tail, never the step time. If TTFT is bad, look at the queue.
- A 2,048-token prefill stalls ~4.5 decode steps at 64 seats.
- Log **average batch**, **queue depth**, **step time**, **p99 TTFT**. Those four explain nearly every serving problem.
- Never ship raw priority: it is starvation with a nicer name.
