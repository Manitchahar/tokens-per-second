## Reading a paper

**Reading order** (never results first):

**title → abstract → setup → method → threats to validity → results**

| Question | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What speed limit does it attack? | title + method | decode is memory-bound, prefill compute-bound |
| Is the hardware mine? | setup | relevance dies here if not |
| Is the workload mine? | setup | batch, context, arrival pattern |
| What does it cost? | method + results | speedup without its memory/latency cost is incomplete |
| What would break it? | threats | the authors' own list; find the one matching you |

Three lenses: **skim** (~10 min, title/abstract/results/conclusion), **deep** (~1 h, + method/setup/threats), **reproduce** (~1 day, + all artifacts).

## The verdict sheet

Five lines, written before any work:

1. **Claim** — one number, one setup, one metric.
2. **Setup match** — same GPU? same model? same workload? what does the difference cost?
3. **Evidence** — is the effect bigger than the noise?
4. **Threats** — which author-listed threat applies to you?
5. **Cost** — memory, latency, quality; and does the code exist?

## Reproducing a result

**The five moves**: pin the claim → pin the setup (or admit you can't) → measure noise first → check the effect → report honestly.

$$
\text{repeats needed} \quad k \approx \left(\frac{2c}{\delta}\right)^2
$$

| Effect δ | c = 5% | c = 8% | c = 10% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4% | 6 | 16 | 25 |
| 6% | 3 | 7 | 11 |
| 10% | 1 | 3 | 4 |
| 20% | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Reduce $c$ before adding repeats — **interleaved A/B runs** are the cheapest lever, and it helps quadratically.

**Three verdicts, all honest:**

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Reproduced** | your band contains the claim |
| **Did not reproduce** | your band excludes it — say why |
| **Inconclusive** | noise too large — say the $k$ you would need |

Bands **overlap** = reproduced. "Close" is not reproduced. Never launder a failure into a vague success.

If you cannot match hardware: reproduce the **mechanism** (measure memory traffic, the prefill-vs-decode asymmetry) instead of the headline number.

## The system design

**Design backwards from the SLO.** Goodput, not raw throughput, is the number that matters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{weights} &= P \times b \\
\text{reserve} &\approx 0.04 \times \text{mem} \\
\text{kv\_per\_tok} &= 2 \times L \times H_{kv} \times d_h \times 2 \\
\text{pool} &= \text{mem} - \text{weights} - \text{reserve} \\
\text{max\_batch} &= \frac{\text{pool}}{\text{ctx} \times \text{kv\_per\_tok}} \\
\text{\$ per M tok} &= \frac{\text{GPU \$/hr}}{\text{goodput tok/s} \times 3600} \times 10^6
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Weights decide what is possible**; the **KV cache decides how many users fit**.
- Context length is a **capacity column**, not a footnote — doubling it roughly halves concurrency.
- Size replicas at **~70% utilization**, with headroom.
- Quantization buys memory and bandwidth at a **measured** quality cost.
- Show every optimization **with its cost**, not just its gain.

## Numbers to derive cold

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| KV bytes/token, Llama-3.1-8B | $2 \times 32 \times 8 \times 128 \times 2 = 128$ KB |
| Decode step, 8B on H100 @ B=1 | $16$ GB ÷ 3.35 TB/s ≈ 5 ms |
| Llama-3.1-70B weights, BF16 | 70.6e9 × 2 = **141 GB** |
| Cost, \$2.50/hr @ 8,000 tok/s | ≈ **\$0.09 / M tokens** |
| Little's law: 4,000 users × 8 s | ≈ **500 in flight** |

If you forget a number, **derive it** on the whiteboard. Watching you reconstruct it is the point.

## The defense

One page: the design in **one defensible sentence**, the evidence (goodput + cost at the SLO load point), the derived arithmetic, the alternatives rejected **with reasons**, and **what would change your mind**.

<Callout type="recap" title="The one sentence">
  *"Serve Llama-3.1-8B in BF16 on A100s with vLLM continuous batching and prefix caching; 220 streams per GPU at 2,048-token context, sized for headroom at 70% utilization; \$0.09 per million good tokens at p95 TTFT under 1 s — and the measurement that would change our mind is doubling context, which halves concurrency and would push us to disaggregation."*
</Callout>
