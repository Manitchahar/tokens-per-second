Six workloads that break the defaults the rest of the course taught you. Each one has a different bottleneck, and each one is wrong somewhere you would expect it to be right.

| workload | unit of work | cache | bound by | the trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| structured output | token, grammar-masked | none needed | neither (mask is ~1–3%) | thinking it costs tokens |
| multi-LoRA | token, per-adapter batch | prefix cache | nothing (footprint) | forgetting batch mixing |
| multimodal | image → 576 tokens | image tokens | prefill (compute) | forgetting the vision tower |
| agents / reasoning | growing prefix | **prefix cache** | decode | quadratic prefill |
| RL rollouts | thousands of whole sequences | none | KV memory | sizing by parameters |
| diffusion | whole image, ×20–50 | feature cache | **compute** | thinking it is memory-bound |

## 1 · Structured output

$$
\text{allowed fraction} = \frac{|\text{tokens legal at this grammar state}|}{|V|}
$$

| state | example | allowed (V = 128,256) |
|---|---|---|
| unconstrained | start | 100% |
| pinned to a character | after a comma, expecting `"` | ~0.04% |
| inside a string | a city name | ~62% |
| enum field | `unit: "c" \| "f"` | ~0.0001% |

- **Cost:** one mask evaluation per step. **Zero extra tokens, zero extra forward passes.**
- A tool call is mostly not information: payload fraction $= 7n/(8 + 12n)$, so **>40% is scaffolding** even with many arguments.
- **Grammar = type system, not a security boundary.** Existence, range, permissions → your validator.
- JSON Schemas cheap, regexes moderate, arbitrary CFGs a project.

## 2 · Multi-LoRA

$$
P_{\text{lora}} = r(in + out) \qquad \text{share} = \frac{r(in+out)}{in \cdot out}
$$

Llama-3.1-8B (32 layers, $d=4096$, FFN 14336, 8 KV heads × 128):

| $r$ | attention only | attn + FFN | share of base | BF16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 6.8M | 16.3M | 0.202% | 33 MB |
| **16** | **13.6M** | **32.5M** | **0.405%** | **65 MB** |
| 32 | 27.3M | 65.0M | 0.810% | 130 MB |
| 64 | 54.5M | 130M | 1.619% | 260 MB |

$$
M_{\text{total}} = P_{\text{base}} b + N \cdot P_{\text{lora}} b
$$

| adapters | total (BF16 base, A100) | base share |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 16.58 GB | 96.9% |
| 16 | 17.10 GB | 93.9% |
| 64 | 20.22 GB | 79.4% |

- **The $r/d$ shorthand understates by 2×** on a square matrix. Always compute $r(in+out)/(in\cdot out)$.
- **Batch mixing:** rows group per adapter into separate GEMMs. Slowdown 1.06× (2 adapters) → 1.47× (8) → 1.96× (16).
- Adapter count per step ≡ batch size. Spreading a batch over tenants is shrinking the batch.
- Adapter FLOPs are 0.4% of the base's — invisible in latency. Cost is memory and batching only.

## 3 · Multimodal

$$
\text{image tokens} = \left(\frac{336}{14}\right)^2 \times \text{crops} \div \text{pooling}
$$

| model | crops | patches | tokens | vision FLOPs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LLaVA-1.5-7B | 1 | 576 | **576** | 1× |
| LLaVA-1.6-7B | 4 | 2,304 | **576** | 4× |

Prefill budget, 576 tokens + 80-token question, H100:

| part | FLOPs | time |
|---|---|---|
| vision tower (0.3B × 576) | 0.35 TFLOP | 0.9 ms |
| language prefill (6.7B × 656) | 8.8 TFLOP | 16 ms |
| projector (~10M) | negligible | ~0 |

- **Any-resolution pooling decouples detail from token cost.** Tokens per image is a design choice.
- **KV cost is permanent:** 576 tokens × 128 KB = **74 MB per image, per sequence, forever.**
- Tower batches over **images**, not sequences — a second bottleneck with its own capacity plan.
- **Cache encoded images by hash of their bytes.** Biggest win available.

## 4 · Agents & reasoning

$$
\text{saved prefill} = 1 - \frac{\sum \text{new tokens}}{\sum \text{context lengths}}
$$

6-turn chat (contexts 960→1640, 840 new tokens): 7,800 → 840 token-prefills = **89% saved**.

$$
\text{agent prefill without cache} = \sum_{t=1}^{T}(s + to) = Ts + o\frac{T(T+1)}{2} = O(T^2)
$$

8 turns, $s=800$, $o=3000$: 24,000 (cached) vs 114,400 → **4.8×**; 20 turns → 646,000 → **10.8×**. Ratio $\approx 2T$.

$$
\text{trace total} = \frac{wT/B + kv\,T^2/2}{\text{BW}\cdot\text{MBU}}
$$

8B BF16 on H100, 128 KB KV/token, MBU 0.85:

| batch | 200 tok | 16,000 tok | 16k takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 177 tok/s | 157 tok/s | 96 s |
| 8 | 1,400 tok/s | 694 tok/s | 17 s |
| 32 | 5,392 tok/s | 1,096 tok/s | 8.7 s |
| 64 | 10,274 tok/s | 1,213 tok/s | 7.3 s |

- **Batch 1 is a trap** — 16 GB read for one token, pinned near 160 tok/s regardless of trace length.
- Weight amortisation ($w/B$) and KV growth are **inseparable effects**; batch decides which dominates.
- Kill the cache hit rate with: a timestamp in the prompt, non-deterministic tool output, swapping adapters mid-conversation.
- Three levers: cap observations, cache the prefix, truncate/summarise old turns.

## 5 · Batch inference & RL rollouts

$$
\text{KV/token} = 2 L H_{kv} D b \qquad \text{KV per rollout} = S \cdot \text{KV/token}
$$

| model | KV/token | KV per 4,096-token rollout |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-1.5B (28L, 2 KV heads) | 28 KB | 0.12 GB |
| Qwen2.5-7B (28L, 4 KV heads) | 56 KB | 0.23 GB |
| Llama-3.1-8B (32L, 8 KV heads) | 128 KB | 0.54 GB |

8× H100 = 640 GB, 16.06 GB weights/card → 511 GB free → **952 rollouts fit**.

| group (4,096 tok) | total KV | share | GPUs of 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | 0.27 TB | 43% | 1 |
| 1,024 | 0.55 TB | 86% | 2 |
| 4,096 | 2.20 TB | 344% | won't fit |

Decode throughput, 8B on one H100 at 4,096 context:

| batch | bytes/step | aggregate | per-seq |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 20 GB | 1,119 tok/s | 140 tok/s |
| 64 | 50 GB | 3,614 tok/s | 56 tok/s |
| 512 | 291 GB | 5,011 tok/s | 10 tok/s |

- **KV is the constraint, not weights** (86% vs 3%). Size by **tokens in flight**, not parameters.
- **Peak memory = group size**, never average. Sizing for average OOMs at step start.
- Batch drains → tail runs at ~¼ peak throughput (that is the batch-8 regime).
- **Cap generation at ~75% of context**, leaving 25% for the reward pass. No cap → the run wedges.

## 6 · Diffusion inference

$$
\text{latent} = \frac{512}{8} = 64,\quad 4 \times 64 \times 64 = 16{,}384 \text{ values}
$$

$$
\text{BW utilisation} = \frac{\text{bytes / peak BW}}{\text{step time}}
$$

SD 1.5, 512², A100 (2.0 TB/s): 2.7 GB per step, 55 ms → 1.35 ms of bandwidth → **2.5% → compute-bound**.

| workload | BW utilisation | bound by |
|---|---|---|
| LLM decode batch 1 | 20–35% | memory |
| LLM decode batch 32 | 60–80% | memory |
| **diffusion step** | **~2%** | **compute** |

Memory (SD 1.5, 512², FP16):

$$
\text{widest tensor} = 320 \times 512 \times 512 \times 2 = 168\text{ MB};\quad \times 6 \approx 1.0\text{ GB/image}
$$

2.1 GB of weights → **activations overtake weights at batch 2.**

| model | steps | s/image (A100) |
|---|---|---|
| SD 1.5 | 20 | 1.2 |
| SD 2.1 | 25 | 1.8 |
| SDXL base | 30 | 4.5 |
| SDXL-Turbo | 4 | 0.8 |

- Latency is **linear in steps**; quality flattens → distilled few-step models win (5.6×).
- **CFG = 2× cost**, always on. Published timings include it.
- Feature caching retains 15–35% of compute, **lossy** (unlike prefix caching).
- Per-image latency is **flat in batch** → run the largest batch that fits. No latency/throughput trade-off.

## The six sentences

1. **Structured output** adds no tokens and no forward passes — one mask per step. It is a type system, not a security boundary, and more than 40% of a tool call is grammar-forced scaffolding.
2. **Multi-LoRA** makes a tenant cost tens of megabytes against a 16 GB base, but adapter count per step is really batch size, and the memory table hides that tax.
3. **Multimodal** is a 576-token prefill with a second model in front; resolution buys vision FLOPs, not context, and the KV cost of an image is permanent.
4. **Agents and reasoning** are a growing prefix with perfect reuse; without a cache prefill is $O(T^2)$, and at batch 1 a long trace is a trap because the weight read dominates everything.
5. **RL rollouts** are sized by tokens in flight, not parameters — KV is 86% of the cluster and peak occupancy is the group size.
6. **Diffusion** is compute-bound at ~2% of peak bandwidth, which inverts every purchasing intuition from the rest of the course, and its memory is activations rather than weights.
